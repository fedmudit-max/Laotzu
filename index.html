    How King Works

    Two problems

    1. When a streak goes to zero, it feels like your progress did too.

    Most recovery apps ask one question: how many days can you stay strong?

    And that's useful. But there's a problem.

    You build a streak. You feel proud. You start feeling like you've beaten the problem.

    Then you slip. The streak goes back to zero. It happens again. And again.

    Soon, you start wondering if the problem isn't the system—it's you.

    You forget the days you stayed strong. The effort. The progress. Everything you've accomplished seems to disappear behind one number.

    You feel like you're starting over. Eventually, you give up. Maybe you even uninstall the app.

    2. Urges can overpower logic.

    We're emotional creatures.

    When a strong urge takes over, emotion can overpower reason. You may know what you should do, but knowing isn't always enough in that moment.

    You need more than willpower. You need a system.

    A system to measure yourself

    We thought progress could be measured differently.

    Don't just count your streak. Measure your Journey.

    Every Journey starts with 10 Powers. Think of your Powers as something valuable—something you don't want to waste.

    Every time you slip, one Power is gone.

    10→9→8→7…

    When you see 9 Powers left, you should feel: “I lost one. I don't want to lose another.”

    That's the idea behind loss aversion: losing something valuable can make us more careful about protecting what remains.

    Your goal is to protect your Powers and see how far you can go before they're gone.

    When all 10 Powers are used, that Journey ends. Then you begin again—with 10 new Powers and a new benchmark to beat.

    A Power can be lost. Your progress cannot.
    One slip may end a streak. It doesn't erase your Journey.

    How far can you go?

    Your Journey Score measures how many strong days you achieve across 10 slips.

    Expecting yourself to maintain a 100-day or one-year streak is like expecting to run a 42 km marathon without training.

    You don't start at 42 km. You start at 1 km.

    Maybe you reach 1 km and fall back. Next time, 1.5 km. Then 2. Then 3. Then 5.

    You might fall back to 3 km—but you've already proved you can reach 5. That's progress.

    Failing is part of the process. The important thing is that you record it and keep moving.

    Our Journey works the same way. Across 10 slips, we measure how far you can go.

    Maybe your first Journey is:

    20 days / 10 slips

    Next time:

    30 days / 10 slips

    That's a 50% improvement.

    You slipped. But you still improved.

    Now 30 days is your personal best. Your next challenge is to go beyond it.

    And the Journey can keep growing:

    50→100→150→200

    A low score shows where your Journey stands today—and gives you a benchmark to improve. It may show you that you're slipping more often than you'd like.

    A score in the hundreds, even with occasional slips, shows just how far you've come.

    You can be imperfect and still be getting stronger. You don't have to be perfect to move forward.

    A slip doesn't erase your Journey. It becomes part of it.

    You can fail forward. Every Journey teaches you something.

    Focus on your Journey. The streak will follow.

    What you need to do

    Keep it simple.

    • Record every strong day.
    • Record every slip.
    • If you relapse once, log it.
    • If you relapse three times in a day, log all three.

    Set a daily reminder to check in and log your day.

    Be honest with your numbers. They show you where you really are.

    Log your strong days and slips honestly. Missed days are counted as strong so you can keep your Journey moving.

    Every Journey teaches you something.

    When you complete a Journey, you begin again. Every Journey gives you a new number to beat.

    The goal isn't to prove that you can go 100 days once. The goal is to become someone who keeps choosing better.

    That's why we don't simply count your streak. We help you understand your Journey.

    What gets measured, gets managed.
