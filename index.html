    How King Works

    Three problems

    1. When zero makes you forget how far you've come

    Most recovery apps ask one question:

    How many days can you stay strong?

    And that's useful. But there's a problem.

    You build a streak. You feel proud. You start feeling like you've beaten the problem.

    Then you slip.

    The streak goes back to zero.

    It happens again. And again.

    Soon, you start wondering if the problem isn't the system — it's you.

    You forget the days you stayed strong. The effort. The discipline. The progress.

    Everything you've accomplished seems to disappear behind one number.

    You feel like you're starting over.

    Eventually, you give up.

    Maybe you even uninstall the app.

    2. Your best days should never become zero

    Imagine reaching 80 days.

    Eighty days of discipline, effort, and choosing yourself every morning.

    Then one day, you slip.

    Your streak becomes zero.

    But those 80 days happened.

    They were real. You did them.

    Yet if the only number the app remembers is your current streak, there's nothing left to remind you what you're capable of.

    The next time an urge hits hard, you can't look back and see the proof.

    King remembers.

    Your milestones are recorded permanently.

    A slip can reset your streak.

    It cannot erase what you've achieved.

    The next time doubt creeps in, King can show you:

    You've done it before. You can do it again.

    3. When the urge is stronger than logic

    Knowing what you should do doesn't always mean you'll do it.

    We're emotional creatures.

    When a strong urge takes over, emotion can overpower reason. In that moment, logic can become surprisingly easy to ignore.

    You may know the consequences.

    You may know you wanted to stay strong.

    But knowing isn't always enough.

    You need more than willpower.

    You need a system that gives you something concrete to protect, measure, and improve.

    A different way to measure progress

    Don't just count your streak.

    Measure your Journey.

    Every Journey starts with 10 Powers.

    Think of your Powers as something valuable — something you don't want to waste.

    Every time you slip, one Power is gone.

    10→9→8→7…

    When you see 9 Powers left, you should feel:

    “I lost one. I don't want to lose another.”

    That's loss aversion — we're naturally motivated to protect something we already have.

    Your goal is to protect your Powers and see how far you can go before they're gone.

    When all 10 Powers are used, that Journey ends.

    Then you begin again — with 10 new Powers and a new benchmark to beat.

    A Power can be lost. Your progress cannot.
    One slip may end a streak.
    It doesn't erase your Journey.

    Your only competition is you

    Most apps measure you against a fixed standard — a perfect streak, an arbitrary goal, or someone else's number.

    King measures you against yourself.

    Your streak shows how you're doing right now.

    Your Journey shows how far you've gone this time.

    Your Personal Best is the highest point you've reached through your own effort.

    That becomes your benchmark.

    Not a leaderboard.

    Not someone else's number.

    Yours.

    Every time you open King, you're not competing against the app.

    You're competing against who you were last Journey.

    That's the only competition worth having.

    You don't start at the bottom again

    Imagine you're climbing an 8,000-metre peak.

    You don't reach the summit in one perfect ascent.

    You climb. You slip. You catch yourself.

    You don't go all the way back to base camp and start over.

    You find your footing and keep climbing.

    Because the altitude you've already gained is real.

    A slip doesn't erase it.

    Now imagine an app that sent you back to base camp every time you slipped.

    Every. Single. Time.

    Eventually, you'd stop climbing.

    Not because the mountain was impossible.

    Because the system was too punishing.

    King works differently.

    When you slip, you lose a Power — not your progress.

    Your strong days remain part of your record. Your Journey still shows how far you've come.

    You find your footing.

    And you keep climbing.

    Because the goal was never to climb without slipping.

    The goal is to reach the summit.

    How far can you go with 10 Powers?

    Your Journey Score measures how many strong days you achieve across 10 slips.

    Expecting yourself to maintain a year-long streak is like expecting to run a 42 km marathon without training.

    You don't start at 42 km.

    You start at 1 km.

    Maybe you reach 1 km and fall back.

    Next time, 1.5 km.

    Then 2.

    Then 3.

    Then 5.

    You might fall back to 3 km — but you've already proved you can reach 5.

    That's progress.

    King's Journey works the same way.

    Across 10 slips, we measure how far you can go.

    Maybe your first Journey is:

    20 days / 10 slips

    Next time:

    30 days / 10 slips

    That's a 50% improvement.

    You slipped.

    But you still improved.

    And now 30 days is your personal best.

    Your next challenge is to go beyond it.

    50→75→100→150

    The Journey can keep growing.

    You don't have to be perfect to become stronger.

    A slip doesn't erase your Journey.

    It becomes part of it.

    You can fail forward.

    Focus on your Journey. The streak will follow.

    The rules are simple

    Keep it simple.

    • Record every strong day.
    • Record every slip.
    • If you slip three times in a day, log all three.

    Be honest with your numbers.

    They show you where you really are.

    Missed days are counted as strong so your Journey keeps moving — but honest logging gives you the clearest picture of where you stand.

    Every Journey gives you a new number to beat.

    When you complete a Journey, you begin again.

    With the same humility.

    With a beginner's mind.

    Because the goal isn't to prove that you can go 100 days once.

    The goal is to become someone who keeps choosing better.

    That's why King doesn't simply count your streak.

    It helps you understand your Journey.

    Measure it.

    Learn from it.

    Improve it.

    What gets measured, gets managed.
